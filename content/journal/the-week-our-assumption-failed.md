# The Week We Found the Real Entry Point

When we first started building Koordi, we were operating under a clear vision: build a portable trust and reputation infrastructure for event commerce.

We believed that if we built a platform that solved the "trust problem," organizers and vendors would naturally flock to it.

And because it felt obvious, the go-to-market strategy felt obvious: sell "trust."

## The Reality Check

We ran outreach campaigns and talked to real event organizers. We asked them how they operated, who they hired, and how they managed the risk of unreliable vendors.

What we discovered completely invalidated our go-to-market assumption—but not our vision.

Professional organizers don't struggle to find trusted vendors on a daily basis. They already have them. Over years of doing business, they have built private, deeply trusted networks. Their business model relies on these existing relationships.

When we tried to sell them "trust," it fell flat. They already had a system for that.

## The Difference Between Product and Distribution

For a moment, it felt like a setback. It felt like the market rejected our product.

But looking back, that week was one of our biggest wins. It taught us the crucial difference between a product vision and a go-to-market strategy.

Our long-term vision—that vendors build a portable reputation, earned through verified work, allowing them to be discovered outside their network—remained exactly the same.

What changed was how we acquire our first users. We realized we couldn't just sell the abstract concept of "trust." We had to sell an immediate, tangible value: **discovery with trust**.

Discovery alone wouldn't work, because a planner won't hire an unverified stranger. We had to offer vendors a way to get discovered while simultaneously proving their reliability through verified histories. That combination is the entry point that allows us to build out the rest of the ecosystem.

## The Lesson

If we hadn't tested our messaging, we would have spent six months building the right product but selling it the wrong way.

This reinforced a critical lesson: **You can have the right vision but the wrong distribution strategy.** And the market will reject both equally until you learn the difference.

We learned the difference. And now we are executing on it.
