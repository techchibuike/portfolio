# What Technical SEO Taught Me About Systems Thinking

Before I was focused on event commerce and trust networks, a significant part of my work involved Technical SEO and building high-performance websites.

Most people view SEO as marketing. They think of it as injecting keywords into blog posts or trying to trick an algorithm into ranking a page higher. But when you dive deep into *Technical* SEO, you realize it isn't marketing at all. 

It is pure systems thinking.

## Auditing the Machine

Search engines are massive, automated coordination systems. Their goal is to organize the world's information and deliver the most relevant result to a specific user at a specific time. 

When I audited a website for technical SEO, I wasn't looking at keywords. I was looking at architecture. 
- How does information flow through this site?
- Where are the bottlenecks?
- Are we forcing the search engine crawler to work too hard to understand what this page is about?
- Is the user experiencing friction before the page even loads?

You quickly learn that you cannot "trick" a well-designed system. The only way to win in SEO is to align your website's architecture with the goals of the search engine and the needs of the user.

## From Websites to Markets

That experience fundamentally changed how I look at businesses and markets today.

A market—like the event industry—is just another complex system. Instead of search engines and web pages, it consists of planners, vendors, and clients trying to coordinate value. 

When planners struggle to find reliable vendors outside their network, that is a bottleneck in the system. When a vendor's reputation is trapped with one planner, that is poor information architecture. 

My job as a founder is exactly the same as my job as a technical SEO specialist: **find the friction in the system, remove it, and align the architecture with what the users are trying to achieve.**

Writing code is just one way to solve the problem. Understanding the system is the real work.
