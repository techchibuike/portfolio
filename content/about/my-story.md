# About Me

## My Story

I'm Damian Chibuike Okpala, a founder and software engineer driven by curiosity and a desire to solve meaningful problems through technology.

My journey into technology began when I chose to study Computer Science. It didn't take long for me to realize that the university curriculum alone wouldn't prepare me for the realities of building software or succeeding in the technology industry. Rather than complain about what was missing, I accepted responsibility for my own growth.

I began learning beyond the classroom through documentation, open-source projects, technical articles, online courses, and countless hours of experimentation. There was no structured roadmap or dedicated mentor guiding me—just curiosity, consistency, and the determination to keep improving.

For several years, much of my life revolved around learning, building, researching, and thinking. That period strengthened my technical skills, but more importantly, it taught me how to learn independently. It developed habits of discipline, patience, persistence, and deep focus that continue to shape the way I work today.

As I gained more experience, my perspective began to change.

At first, I was focused on becoming a better software engineer. Over time, I realized that technology itself isn't the goal—it's a means to solve meaningful problems. That realization changed the questions I asked.

Instead of asking only, **"Can I build this?"**, I began asking, **"Should this exist?"**

That shift moved my attention beyond writing code. I became increasingly interested in understanding people, businesses, workflows, and the systems behind the problems technology aims to solve. Today, I believe the best products are built by understanding reality first and using technology intentionally to create value.

---

## How I Think

I'm naturally curious, but I'm even more interested in understanding than in simply collecting knowledge.

When I encounter a problem, I rarely jump straight into building a solution. My instinct is to understand the system behind it.

I ask questions like:

* Why does this problem exist?
* Who experiences it?
* How do people solve it today?
* What assumptions are shaping the current solution?
* Where does friction actually occur?
* Is technology the right answer, or just the most obvious one?

This way of thinking has gradually expanded my perspective beyond software engineering alone. Today, I'm just as interested in customer discovery, product strategy, business models, and market understanding as I am in writing code.

I've learned that great products don't begin with technology—they begin with understanding.

---

## What I Believe

### Build understanding before building products.

Every product starts with assumptions. The best solutions come from understanding people, their workflows, and the problems they actually experience before building technology.

### Every assumption needs evidence.

Ideas are only hypotheses until they're tested. Conversations, observation, and experimentation matter more than confidence.

### Visibility compounds opportunity.

For a long time, I believed good work would naturally speak for itself. Experience has shown me that relationships, communication, and sharing what you're learning create opportunities that technical ability alone cannot.

### Learning is a competitive advantage.

Technology changes constantly. The ability to learn, adapt, and rethink assumptions is one of the most valuable skills I can develop.

### Technology is a means, not the destination.

I'm passionate about technology because of what it enables. My goal isn't simply to build software—it's to create products that improve the way people live, work, and solve problems.

---

## What I'm Working Towards

My long-term vision is to build technology-driven companies that solve meaningful problems at scale.

That journey is currently taking shape through Koordi, where I'm learning how customer discovery, product thinking, software engineering, and business strategy come together to build products people genuinely need.

Every conversation, experiment, success, and setback helps me refine not only what I'm building, but also how I think as a founder.

Ultimately, I don't want to be known simply as someone who writes software.

I want to be known as someone who understands complex problems, brings people together to solve them, and builds technology that creates lasting value.
