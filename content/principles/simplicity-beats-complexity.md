# Simplicity Beats Complexity

As engineers, we are often drawn to complexity. We admire systems with highly abstracted architectures, microservices, and cutting-edge technologies. We sometimes conflate "complex" with "valuable."

But to a user, complexity is friction. 

## The Principle

**The best solutions remove friction; they do not add new steps.**

## The Burden of Adoption

When you introduce a new tool into someone's workflow, you are asking them to change their behavior. If the tool is complex, the barrier to adoption is incredibly high. 

If an event organizer currently uses a simple WhatsApp group to coordinate vendors, and you ask them to switch to a complex project management dashboard requiring five steps to send a single message, they will not use it. It doesn't matter how robust the software is behind the scenes.

Your product must be easier to use than the workaround they currently employ. 

## Engineering for Simplicity

Simplicity is much harder to engineer than complexity. It requires you to deeply understand the core problem and ruthlessly cut away anything that doesn't solve it.

- Don't build five features when one will do.
- Don't require users to configure settings if you can choose a smart default.
- Hide the technical complexity behind a simple, intuitive interface.

The value of software is not determined by how hard it was to build. It is determined by how easy it is to use.
