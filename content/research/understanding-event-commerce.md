# Understanding Event Commerce: A Research Summary

## Why This Research Existed

Before building products, we needed to understand how the market already works.

Technology often assumes markets are waiting for software to solve their problems. People rarely wait for technology. They develop their own processes, relationships, and workarounds.

Understanding those systems was essential before attempting to improve them.

## Our Initial Assumption

When we first explored the event industry, we believed trust between organizers and vendors was the primary challenge.

The assumption was simple:

- Organizers struggled to identify reliable vendors.
- Vendors struggled to demonstrate credibility.
- A platform could become the missing trust layer.

We treated this as a hypothesis—not a fact.

## What We Learned

Our research revealed something we had not anticipated.

Professional planners already have a functioning trust system.

Over time they build private vendor networks based on repeated experience.

These networks become their operating system.

When clients hire a planner, they are also hiring access to that trusted network.

The planner becomes the trust layer between clients and vendors.

## The Workaround

The market had not ignored the trust problem. Instead, experienced professionals had developed their own solution.

Private vendor networks solve trust remarkably well.

However, they remain informal systems built on personal relationships.

That naturally introduces limitations.

## The Structural Constraints

Private trust networks work because they are personal. They also struggle because they are personal.

| Constraint | What It Means |
|:---|:---|
| Scalability | A planner can only personally maintain so many relationships |
| Transferability | The network dies when the planner leaves |
| Visibility | The vendor's reputation is invisible outside the network |

## Where Koordi Fits

Koordi is not a replacement for private networks. It is the public equivalent of what private networks already provide.

| Private Network | Koordi |
|:---|:---|
| Closed | Open |
| Personal | Structured |
| Non-transferable | Portable |
| Capped by the planner's capacity | Scalable |

Koordi does not replace the relationships planners have built. It makes reputation portable, visible, and scalable.

## Key Insight

The market does not have a trust problem.

It has a scalability problem.

Trust exists. It is just private. And private trust does not scale.

Koordi is not replacing private networks. It is building the public infrastructure that makes trust portable, visible, and scalable.
